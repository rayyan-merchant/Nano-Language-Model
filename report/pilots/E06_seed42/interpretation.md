## Learning and predictive performance

E06 has lower validation loss at 19 of 20 matched checkpoints. At 2000 it is
slightly higher: 1.76129042 versus 1.75925914, a 0.00203128-nat difference.
Both runs select update4750. E06 improves best loss by 0.00601516 nats
(0.39195%) and best BPB by 0.00867804. Final loss improves by 0.00680776
nats (0.44321%). See [all twenty matched points](comparison/matched_validation.csv).
Four heads offer a small observed advantage here, with no significance estimate
or claim that the result generalizes to other widths, depths or seeds.

The wider head dimension48 could change representation grouping; no attention
specialization measurement establishes why these scores differ. Projection
widths, parameters and initial values match. Sampling RNG and source coverage
match. Different attention tensor shapes and dropout draws can still produce
different optimization trajectories despite the shared seed.

Validation decreases by 0.12850636 over2k-3k, 0.06532372 over3k-4k and
0.03825600 over4k-5k. The3k-5k decrease is0.10357971: diminishing returns
with meaningful continued improvement, not a demonstrated flat plateau.
The final loss is only0.00055149 above its4750 minimum. This small reversal
does not establish sustained overfitting or double descent. Final trailing
100-update training loss is1.47807136 versus1.52567231 at4k. The final
validation-minus-trailing-training gap is about0.051133 nats, but training
dropout/random windows and validation evaluation-mode/sliding scoring differ.
The gap cannot be treated as an isolated memorization measure.

Seven pre-clipping gradient norms exceed1.0 (0.14%); every LR is0.0003.
The journal has no failure/resume event. All20 checkpoints verify optimizer and
scheduler progression. Float32 was used throughout; test likelihood was not
computed. These checks confirm the executed protocol, not across-seed robustness.

## Genuine generation and failures

[All100 unedited samples](generation.md) cover best/final, five prompts,
greedy seed42, temperatures0.7/1.0/1.2 and seeds42/123/456,256 new characters.
The primary report includes all five predetermined best greedy examples.
Best greedy ROMEO begins "I would not so do the so die to the soul" and
falls into repeated "seem" and "world" constructions. JULIET begins
"The shall be so death the so death". Multiple prompts produce DUKE OF YORK
or Second Servingman labels and repeated turns. Line breaks, speaker labels and
recognizable vocabulary coexist with weak syntax, weak character identity and
poor sustained semantic consistency. Final ROMEO repeats "so" many times.
Validation-best selection does not guarantee useful greedy decoding.

Predetermined temperature0.7 seed42 outputs reduce loops but contain "meddiness",
"distred", "prevate" and "confeing", with agreement and sentence errors.
"The king" becomes "The kingdom", showing literal continuation rather than
reliable semantic instruction following. Higher-temperature samples retain
dialogue layout but contain malformed words and unstable labels; final
temperature1.2 seed456 includes "TYRRBALT". No text was edited or generated by
an external language model.

Mean repeated8-character-window position fraction across five best greedy
continuations is0.447390 forE06 versus0.522892 forE01. Best temperature0.7
fractions are0.005355 versus0.001339. The direction depends on decoding;
this descriptive measure ignores grammar and semantics and establishes no
global quality ranking. All continuations are256 characters; no replacement
characters were found. [Every matched setting](generation_quality.json) is kept.
A predetermined subset of ten best outputs has longest exact training-text
overlaps of13-24 characters, often labels or ordinary phrases. This limited
check neither proves nor excludes memorization and is not a corpus-wide test.
